## Epistemic vs. Aleatoric Uncertainty

When deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) in high-stakes domains—such as legal retrieval or clinical diagnostic routing—mistakes are highly asymmetrical. A hallucinated fact or confidence miscalibration can carry severe liability. To manage this risk, we must frame model reliability in terms of uncertainty.

Specifically, we target **epistemic uncertainty** (uncertainty in the model's knowledge) rather than **aleatoric uncertainty** (stochastic noise). Our goal is to define an optimal decision rule where the model abstains:

<Math math="f(x) = \begin{cases} y & \text{if } P(y \text{ is correct} \mid x) \ge \theta \\ \emptyset & \text{otherwise (abstain)} \end{cases}" block />

## Quantifying Uncertainty via Self-Consistency

We approximate epistemic confidence by sampling from the model's output distribution at elevated temperature <Math math="\tau > 0" />. Following Wang et al. (2022), we generate <Math math="k" /> independent reasoning paths and compute the entropy of the resulting answers:

<Math math="H(Y \mid X) = -\sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} \hat{P}(y \mid X) \log \hat{P}(y \mid X)" block />

Where <Math math="\hat{P}(y \mid X) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k \mathbb{I}(y_i = y)" /> is the empirical probability of answer <Math math="y" />. Higher entropy indicates a flat distribution, signifying epistemic fragility.

## Calibration and Platt Scaling

Raw self-consistency counts are poorly calibrated. To map the self-consistency signals and token-level log-likelihoods to a true probability of correctness, we apply **Platt Scaling** (Platt 1999). We train a logistic calibration layer on a small validation set of human-annotated outputs:

<Math math="P(\text{correct} \mid S) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(A \cdot S + B)}" block />

Here, <Math math="S" /> is a feature vector containing consistency metrics, token entropy, and adversarial probe scores. Setting <Math math="\theta" /> based on calibrated output probabilities allows enterprise systems to guarantee a maximum error rate, successfully deciding when to route to human specialists.
